## Where the evidence is strongest

HVAC, plumbing and electrical firms show the clearest, checkable signals. The strongest leads have a public promise or published hours that a small team can't always keep.

The main caveat is existing software. Five of the 9 HVAC, plumbing and electrical firms already run ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber or GoHighLevel. Each of these sells AI call answering or automatic text-back, so for those firms ForgeX should measure and configure, not build.

## Services most feasible for AI-based delivery

The Revenue Leak Check is the easiest to deliver with AI and fits all seven contact-first firms. Building new systems comes last.

1. **Revenue Leak Check.** Call records, booking exports and lead-tracking data go in, and a one-page finding comes out. AI does the counting and drafting; you review it and present it. It's fastest where the data already exists, as at Running Water Plumbing.
2. **Setting up what they already pay for.** For the five firms on ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber or GoHighLevel, the honest fix is to switch on and test the vendor's own answering or text-back. AI prepares the setup checklist and test-call scripts. The work is small and low-risk, and it builds trust.
3. **Lead-to-job pipeline for firms with no platform.** This means missed-call text-back, after-hours requests and alerts for firms such as Crofts, Walker, Gem and Kings. It's feasible but carries the most delivery risk: phone forwarding or porting, and US carrier registration for business texting (A2P 10DLC).
4. **Owner's Monday numbers.** It works where a field-service system exports data. It could suit the well-run firms on hold, such as Plumbing Solutions and Boise Remodeling, if they want it.
5. **Manufacturing quote intake.** This is the least feasible now, because of drawings, ERP quoting modules and possibly export-controlled data. There's no offer until discovery calls show a repeatable need.

## What the pilot shows about research quality

Public research is good at verifying a company and spotting signals. It can't confirm a problem or put a value on it, and a third of the apparent website flaws were false.

- **Official records worked for the trades.** Idaho's DOPL search confirmed active licenses or registrations for 12 of the 15 construction and home-service firms. The 3 misses publish no license number. Name search is fuzzy, so each lookup needs the exact registered name. Two records carry a discipline flag, which I noted but kept out of outreach.
- **Reading page text misses software.** A scan of the page source found ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, WhatConverts and a chat widget on four sites, plus an email address on a fifth, where reading the page text had found none of them. Five sites refused automated requests, and I didn't try to get around that.
- **Website flaws need a real browser.** Of six apparent flaws, two held up in a browser: Cornerstone's email links and North Star's mistyped button. Three were artifacts (two animated counters that read 0 and some hidden placeholder text), and one couldn't be tested.
- **Locations need checking.** One of the six manufacturing candidates was a Wisconsin shop with a "Nampa" landing page. Another's site was down, and a third never gives an address.
- **Names are scarce.** I found a decision-maker's name for 12 of 25 companies: 9 from company sites and 3 from license records. None was guessed.
- **Value can't be estimated from public data.** Without call or quote volumes, no customer value was defensible; only ForgeX's own prices are.
- **Effort.** The pilot took about 70 research actions, roughly 3 per company. Run as ForgeX's own research step, that suggests $0.10–$0.25 per company at current Claude API prices (an estimate).

## Minimum additions to ForgeX HQ

Make the process repeatable first, then let ForgeX run the research itself. Until step 6, research still depends on a Claude Code session like this one, not on ForgeX.

**Without new spend**

1. **Prospect records** in the existing database: companies, evidence with its label, source and date, contacts with the allowed channel, drafts, your decisions and outcomes. This pilot's file imports as the first batch.
2. **A review page in HQ.** It shows the ranked list with the three score parts and a dossier view with sources. You mark each prospect Contact, Hold or Reject with a reason, then log what happens: sent by you, replied, call held, Leak Check bought, setup bought. A do-not-contact list blocks new drafts.
3. **A page-source check.** One request per homepage, robots.txt honored, flagging known booking, tracking and phone tools. It needs no AI, and it caught what reading missed.
4. **Playbook files** for home services, residential construction, manufacturing and IT services. They carry this pilot's rules: confirm addresses, check websites in a browser, never cite counters, existing software first.
5. **Pilot metrics** on the same page. Decisions by industry and score band come first; replies, calls and sales by score follow as outcomes arrive. That's how qualification accuracy, source reliability and cost per useful prospect get measured.

**Needs your approval**

6. **ForgeX's own research step.** One server task uses the Claude API's web search and fetch, with restricted sites blocked, structured output and the cost logged for each company. It needs an Anthropic API key with a monthly cap. Estimate: $0.10–$0.25 per company, about $10–$25 per 100 companies.

Not needed: agent frameworks, a separate database, paid data services or scraping.

## Limits of this pilot

- No business has confirmed any problem. Every pain point here is a hypothesis.
- Company size is unknown for most firms; there are only clues.
- Not checked: Secretary of State records, and by policy, review sites, LinkedIn, Google Maps and job boards.
- Region capacity is unknown. Census business counts now need an API key, which is free but needs you to register. So it's unclear whether the Treasure Valley holds enough fitting firms for the roughly 850 contacts the cold path needs. DOPL licensee lists come through a written public-records request, and the fee for contractor lists isn't published.
- Drafts use placeholders for your name, phone and the ForgeX postal address, which aren't decided yet.
